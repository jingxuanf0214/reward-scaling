Next-token Preference: Scaling Unsupervised Reward Modeling

Revised October 5, 2026

Jingxuan Fan 1, Yueying Li 2, Zhenting Qi 1, Dinghuai Zhang 3,
Kianté Brantley 1,4, Sham M. Kakade 1,4, Hanlin Zhang 1
1Harvard University    2Cornell University    3Microsoft Research    4Kempner Institute
Paper arXiv Code

Can raw web text teach a model what makes a response better? Next-token preference (NTP) turns the continuation structure of existing text into reward-model supervision, without generating candidates or collecting human or model-generated preference labels.

  • Transferable rewards: 11M tokens per corpus yield peak overall RewardBench 2 gains of 7.6–8.3 percentage points across mathematical and general-domain web text.
  • Downstream utility: the learned rewards improve response selection in math, code, safety, and instruction following, and support math policy optimization.
  • Supervised training: NTP provides a useful initialization for reward learning from curated preference data.

Motivation

Reward models guide response selection and policy optimization in reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) [1]. Conventional preference pipelines require candidate generation and judgments from people or models [2], [3], [4]. Automating the judging step still leaves generation and scoring costs.

Human and model preferences can also be noisy or inconsistent [5], [6], [7], [8]. This motivates a question: how much useful reward supervision is already present in the structure of web text? NTP uses the relationship between a prefix and its original continuation as an implicit preference signal. All comparisons come from existing text, so constructing them requires neither new responses nor explicit preference annotations.

Method: Next-token Preference

We concatenate documents into a continuous token stream and divide it into fixed-length blocks. Each block becomes a prefix \(x\) and its original continuation \(y\). Grouping \(B\) nearby chunks into a batch gives one chosen continuation per prefix and \(B-1\) rejected continuations from the other chunks.

Nearby negatives matter. They can share topic, vocabulary, and style with the true continuation while breaking the semantic or logical dependencies across the split. Ranking these alternatives encourages the reward model to learn continuation consistency. Each batch supplies \(B(B-1)\) preference comparisons assembled online.

For a scalar reward \(s_\theta(x,y)\), the Bradley–Terry objective ranks each original continuation above the in-batch alternatives:

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BT}} = -\frac{1}{B(B-1)} \sum_{i=1}^{B}\sum_{j\ne i} \log\sigma\!\left(s_\theta(x_i,y_i)-s_\theta(x_i,y_j)\right). \]

We add a quadratic score-centering penalty to limit reward-scale drift and overly large margins:

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{center}} = \mathbb{E}\!\left[ s_\theta(x_i,y_i)^2 + \frac{1}{B-1}\sum_{j\ne i}s_\theta(x_i,y_j)^2 \right]. \]

The final objective is \(\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BT}}+c\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{center}}\), with \(c=0.01\) in the reported recipe. The split can occur mid-sentence; preserving sentence boundaries is an ablation, not a requirement.

Experimental Protocol

Training data. Our main corpus is FineMath-4plus. We also test MegaMath-Web-Pro-Max (mathematical web text) and FineWeb-Edu (general educational web text), using 11M tokens per corpus. The default split uses a 512-token prefix and a 1,024-token continuation.

Backbones. We train base Llama-3.2-1B and Llama-3.2-3B models, plus instruction-tuned Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct models. Llama-3.2-3B is the controlled-ablation backbone. The default batch size is 32; Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct uses 8.

Preference alignment. We report overall RewardBench and RewardBench 2 accuracy, alongside:

  • In-domain (ID): RewardBench Reasoning (math and code) and RewardBench 2 Math.
  • OOD Safety: the Safety subset of each benchmark.
  • OOD Others: the remaining subsets, including conversational quality in RewardBench and factuality, ties, focus, and precise instruction following in RewardBench 2.

Downstream evaluation. Best-of-N selection tests math, code, safety, and instruction following with 1B, 3B, and 8B actors. GRPO tests the reward signal for math policy training. Finally, curated-preference training tests NTP as an initialization for supervised reward learning.

Data Scaling and Transfer

Reward learning improves as more web text is used. With Llama-3.2-3B, the overall trend across the 11M-token budget is upward on both benchmarks, with gains extending beyond mathematical reasoning. At 11M tokens, RewardBench Safety improves by 16.0 percentage points over initialization, compared with 13.1 points for Reasoning and 7.0 points for Chat Hard.

The recipe transfers across model families and sizes. All four trained models rank in the top 10 on RewardBench 2 within parameter-matched cohorts (0.5–1.5B, 3–4B, and 7B).

Comparison with Curated Preferences

At the same 11M-token budget, NTP produces larger RewardBench 2 gains than training on the tested curated preference datasets. FineMath NTP improves overall accuracy by 7.7 percentage points, compared with 1.8 for Skywork-80K-v0.2 and 0.8 for RLHFMix1. Training on the full curated datasets remains stronger, but requires substantially more labeled data.

Recipe Ablations

Batch size

Increasing batch size expands the number of in-batch comparisons and improves peak overall RewardBench 2 gains. ID gains continue to grow with batch size, while OOD gains show little benefit beyond \(B=16\). This also increases scoring work because the number of comparisons grows quadratically.

Splitting format

Allowing a split within a sentence produces stronger gains. Enforcing sentence boundaries discards candidate spans during packing and leaves less contextually similar negatives. Contiguous chunks preserve more shared context, making mismatched continuations harder to reject through topic or style alone.

Score centering

Centering improves peak overall RewardBench 2 gain from 6.4 to 7.7 points. Its larger benefit is in downstream selection: on GSM8K with the Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct actor, the gain in best-of-N Maximum Achieved Performance (ΔMAP) increases from 2.8 to 5.4 percentage points. Without centering, accuracy peaks at small N and eventually falls below random selection.

Does More Next-token Training Explain the Gains?

We continue pretraining Llama-3.2-3B on the same 11M FineMath tokens in the same order, using next-token prediction. To read out reward quality, we fit a frozen-backbone linear probe or jointly train a scalar reward head and backbone on 512 auxiliary preference pairs from held-out FineMath blocks. These pairs compare original continuations with perturbed copies.

Continued pretraining (CPT) does not improve RewardBench 2 performance over the untouched base model under either readout. In contrast, replacing NTP’s learned head with the same frozen-backbone probe preserves its reward performance. This supports a contribution from the preference-learning objective beyond additional exposure to the corpus.

Generalization to Other Web Corpora

NTP also works beyond FineMath. Under the same Llama-3.2-3B initialization, training settings, and 11M-token budget per corpus, MegaMath-Web-Pro-Max gives the largest overall gain. General-domain FineWeb-Edu comes within 0.1 percentage points of FineMath, showing that useful reward supervision also exists in educational text outside a math-specific corpus.

Best-of-N Selection

We sample \(N\in\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}\) responses per prompt and select the one with the highest reward. MAP is Maximum Achieved Performance, the best task accuracy across the tested values of N. Actors are Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.

We evaluate MATH500 and GSM8K (math), HumanEval+ (code), ToxiGen (safety), and IFEval (instruction following). The two NTP models are FineMath-RM-Llama-3.2-3B and FineMath-RM-Qwen-2.5-7B. Baselines include the Skywork 3B and 8B reward models, the supervised safety-oriented beaver-7b-unified-reward, and random selection.

Selection gains become more consistent with stronger actors. The Qwen-based NTP model exceeds Beaver’s MAP on every evaluated task and actor size. It also exceeds Skywork’s 3B RM on ToxiGen at every actor size and matches Skywork’s 8B RM on MATH500 with the 8B actor. The strongest curated-data RMs remain ahead overall, and improvements are not monotonic in every setting.

Policy Optimization with GRPO

We train instruction-tuned 3B and 8B Llama actors on MATH and GSM8K for five GRPO epochs, using FineMath-RM-Qwen-2.5-7B as the reward signal. We report peak mean@1 test accuracy within that budget.

Our RM improves both actors on both tasks and achieves the best or second-best result in three of four settings, including the best GSM8K result for the 8B actor. It exceeds Beaver by 0.4–2.0 percentage points and outperforms its own untrained initialization in all four settings.

NTP as an Initialization for Supervised Training

Unlabeled web text can make subsequent preference labels more useful. We compare base, instruction-tuned, and NTP-trained Llama-3.2-3B initializations, then train each on RLHFMix1 [9], a mixture of UltraFeedback, HH-RLHF, SHP, and Summarize-from-Feedback.

The NTP checkpoint achieves a reported overall RewardBench 2 gain of 10.4 points after curated training, compared with 8.3 for base and 13.1 for instruction-tuned initialization. NTP gives the strongest reported ID-Math and OOD-Safety gains, while instruction-tuned initialization remains stronger overall and on OOD Others.
